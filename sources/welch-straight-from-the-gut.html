Overview

Jack: Straight from the Gut is Jack Welch’s first-person account, written with John A. Byrne, of a career at General Electric from the plastics pit through the chairmanship. Welch narrates childhood and education, early GE plastics entrepreneurship, the succession race to replace Reg Jones, and the CEO years defined by the no. 1 or no. 2 portfolio rule, delayering, “boundaryless” behavior, Crotonville teaching, Work-Out, differentiation of people, globalization, services, Six Sigma, and the late succession contest—ending as he leaves the company.

For this United States 1946–89 wiki the highest-value material is Welch’s formation and 1981–89 restructuring: GE culture before and after he takes the chair (April 1981), plant closings and “Neutron Jack” reputation, portfolio surgery in the early Reagan years, Crotonville as corporate university, and the emerging boundaryless/Work-Out ideology by the late 1980s. Childhood through plastics (1950s–70s) supply corporate-career social history. Chapters on 1990s services, NBC deal aftermath beyond the period edge, Six Sigma (1995+), Internet, and succession are post-1989 spill—use for continuity of 1980s doctrines, not as primary 1946–89 evidence.
