Overview

David Sarnoff is Eugene Lyons’s life of the RCA chief—immigrant childhood through electronics leadership: ghetto childhood in Russia; New York slums and Marconi messenger/operator years (including Titanic survivor traffic); commercial manager and “radio music box” prophecy; creation of the Radio Corporation of America; NBC networks and talkie/phonograph alliances; Depression research stubbornness and the fight for television; World War II mobilization and European theatre service; postwar “father of television,” color-TV triumph, setbacks, public-figure roles, and still-active eighth decade as of the mid-1960s.

For this United States 1946–89 wiki the core is Chapters 20–26: postwar TV industry leadership, color struggle, corporate succession (Robert Sarnoff), failed crusades, public-figure honors, and 1950s–60s electronics stature. Chapters 1–19 are continuity from earlier U.S. period wikis (especially 1920–45). Period-gate hard on pre-1946 narrative except where needed to finish stories already under way after 1945.

PDF structure: jacket/title/copyright; dedication; Contents (Chs. 1–26 + Index); Chapters 1–26; Index; residual jacket blurbs.

Front matter

Lines 1–301. Jacket copy; title; books by Lyons; Harper & Row 1966 copyright (LC 66-10632); dedication to grandchildren; Contents.

Chapter 1 — Success Story

Lines 302–766. Frames Sarnoff as rags-to-riches classic of the electronic age—radio, TV, satellites, gadgetry—rather than merely another fortune tale.

Chapter 2 — Twin of the Electron

Lines 767–1286. Birth of wireless as long scientific arc; Sarnoff’s life paired with the electron’s industrial coming-of-age.

Chapter 3 — Boyhood in the Promised Land

Lines 1287–1901. Immigrant arrival; New York “promised land” boyhood and early American weeks.

Chapter 4 — The Formative Years

Lines 1902–2497. Marconi Wireless Telegraph Company apprenticeship; formative wireless-operator years.

Chapter 5 — The Titanic and the Music Box

Lines 2498–3191. 1912 voting-age year; Titanic wireless traffic fame; early “radio music box” vision for broadcasting.

Chapter 6 — The Radio Corporation Is Born

Lines 3192–3774. Commercial manager role; RCA’s birth from Marconi/GE wartime communications complex.

Chapter 7 — “Sarnoff’s Folly”

Lines 3775–4380. Early RCA push for broadcasting dismissed as folly; Sarnoff’s energy inside the new corporation.

Chapter 8 — The Self-Appointed Leader

Lines 4381–4875. After age thirty (May 1921), self-assertion as industry leader.

Chapter 9 — Prophet with Honor

Lines 4876–5517. Forecasts of industry growth; forums with Hoover-era figures; prophet reputation.

Chapter 10 — Networks and Talkies

Lines 5518–5995. Private life (Lizette; sons Robert, Edward, Thomas) beside network building and phonograph/talkie alliances.

Chapter 11 — A New President Is Born

Lines 5996–6593. Phonograph–radio “marriage”; path to RCA presidency.

Chapter 12 — Double Trouble

Lines 6594–7119. Presidency amid Depression dual crises for radio industry and company.

Chapter 13 — The Roosevelt Years

Lines 7120–7908. Depression research as first ballast to cut—Sarnoff refuses to jettison R&D under bookkeeping pressure.

Chapter 14 — Quest for Quality

Lines 7909–8535. September 1937 board/quality drive; programming and technical standards agenda.

Chapter 15 — Struggle for Television

Lines 8536–9219. Prewar and wartime TV development fight; antenna forest as postwar symbol introduced retrospectively.

Chapter 16 — At the Half-Century Mark

Lines 9220–9648. WWII decorations and half-century career mark framing.

Chapter 17 — Another World War

Lines 9649–10037. 7 Dec 1941 offer of RCA facilities to FDR; electronics switch to military radio, radar, tubes, navigation.

Chapter 18 — In the European Theatre

Lines 10038–10528. Near age fifty-one; wartime service in the European theatre.

Chapter 19 — D Day and After

Lines 10529–11031. Allied invasion and Sarnoff’s wartime communications role through war’s end.

Chapter 20 — Father of Television

Lines 11032–11646. Dec 1944 Television Broadcasters Association award of the title “Father of Television”; postwar TV push—wiki core begins.

Chapter 21 — Roster of Setbacks

Lines 11647–12291. Late-1940s setbacks (Newsweek Dec 1949 cover era); competitive and regulatory bruises.

Chapter 22 — Triumph for Color

Lines 12292–12716. Color television hammered out at Princeton labs; 1951 forty-five-year association celebration.

Chapter 23 — Tribute in Gold

Lines 12717–13230. Dec 1955: Robert Sarnoff named RCA president/CEO; succession tribute.

Chapter 24 — The Crusade That Failed

Lines 13231–13575. Firm opinions on domestic/world problems; a public crusade that fails.

Chapter 25 — The “Public Figure”

Lines 13576–14173. FDR-era request beginnings of public-service persona; postwar public-figure stature.

Chapter 26 — In the Eighth Decade

Lines 14174–14638. 1961 Capitol Hill luncheon honor (55th anniversary with radio); still-active eighth decade as Lyons writes.
