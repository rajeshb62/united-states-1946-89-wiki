Alleged Assassination Plots Involving Foreign Leaders (Church Committee Interim Report)

Overview

This interim report is the Church Committee’s public case study of alleged U.S. government involvement in assassination plots and closely related covert action against foreign leaders, issued months before the April 1976 final report books. It combines a general discussion of covert action as a foreign-policy instrument with detailed narratives on the Congo (Patrice Lumumba), Cuba (Fidel Castro, underworld plots, Operation MONGOOSE, and “executive action” capability), the Dominican Republic (Rafael Trujillo), South Vietnam (Ngo Dinh Diem), and Chile (General René Schneider), then states findings, conclusions, and recommendations, with chronology and member views.

For this United States 1946–89 wiki the interim report is a primary source on Cold War covert action as Congress reconstructed it in 1975: Eisenhower–Kennedy–Johnson–Nixon-era plotting and contingency planning; CIA relationships with underworld figures; Special Group / 303 Committee / 40 Committee authorization patterns as the Committee describes them; and the mid-1970s political reckoning over assassination as an instrument. The lens is the Committee’s own: factual reconstruction from documents and testimony, degrees of authorization and ambiguity, and normative conclusions against political assassination. It is not a substitute for Book I’s broader covert-action chapter or for Hearings Volume 7 on Chile; it is the specialized assassination dossier.

Narrative Source Flow

Prologue

Sets the moral and institutional stakes of investigating assassination allegations under the Select Committee’s mandate, before the technical introduction.

I. Introduction and Summary

States the Committee’s charge, the decision to issue an interim public report on assassination, the evidentiary base (documents, sworn testimony, executive-session material as authorized for release), and a condensed summary of what the case studies show about plots, authorization, and results. Frames definitions (assassination versus coups, support for dissidents, contingency planning) as the Committee uses them.

II. Covert Action as a Vehicle for Foreign Policy Implementation

Places assassination allegations inside the larger postwar covert-action system: clandestine influence on foreign governments, organizations, persons, or events; presidential and NSC-level approval mechanisms; CIA operational role; and the policy problem of means that remain deniable. Bridges general covert-action practice to the specific plots that follow.

III. Assassination Planning and Plots

The case-study core of the interim report.

A. Congo

Narrative on U.S. interest in the Congo crisis after independence, concern over Lumumba, CIA planning and steps directed at Lumumba’s removal or death as the Committee reconstructs them, and the relationship between U.S. actions and Lumumba’s eventual death. Subsections in the Contents treat the plot structure and operational detail.

B. Cuba

Longest country dossier: early anti-Castro planning; underworld (mafia) poison and related plots; post–Bay of Pigs continuation; Operation MONGOOSE under the Kennedy administration; amnesia and testimony conflicts among principals; and the Committee’s reconstruction of what presidents and senior officers knew or authorized. Includes discussion of plots that did not succeed and of the bureaucratic trail.

C. Institutionalizing Assassination: The “Executive Action” Capability

Treats development of a standing capability or project (often discussed under cryptonyms such as ZR/RIFLE in the wider public record; the report’s own labels govern) intended to provide a capability for assassination or related “executive action,” separate from but related to the country plots.

D. Trujillo

U.S. relationships with Dominican dissidents and the assassination of Rafael Trujillo (1961): arms, encouragement, knowledge, and the Committee’s distinctions among levels of U.S. involvement.

E. Diem

Coup against Ngo Dinh Diem and Ngo Dinh Nhu (1963): U.S. policy toward a change of government in Saigon, cables and authorization, and the Committee’s findings on whether assassination of the Diem brothers was planned or supported as such versus a coup that ended in their deaths.

F. Schneider

Chile 1970: Track I/Track II themes as the Committee presents them, the kidnapping and death of Army Commander-in-Chief René Schneider, CIA contact with Chilean officers, weapons, and the relationship between Schneider’s death and the failure to block Allende’s confirmation. Connects to the later Chile staff report and Hearings Volume 7 without replacing them.

IV. Findings and Conclusions

Committee judgments across the cases: whether U.S. officials initiated or were involved in plots to assassinate foreign leaders; patterns of implied authorization and failure to halt plotting; distinction among successful killings, unsuccessful plots, and coups resulting in death; responsibility of presidents, DCI-level officers, and operational elements as the evidence allowed the senators to state it; and general conclusions about assassination as an instrument of U.S. policy.

V. Recommendations

Legislative and executive recommendations arising from the interim investigation (including proposed statutory language referenced to Appendix A), aimed at prohibiting assassination of foreign leaders and tightening control of related covert activities—pending the fuller final-report recommendations in Books I–II.

Epilogue and Statement of Joinder

Closing institutional notes; statement of joinder by members associating themselves with the interim report’s issuance.

Appendix A

Recommended statutory language (and related appendix material) supporting Part V.

Chronology of Major Events

Timeline of principal events across the Congo, Cuba, Trujillo, Diem, Schneider, and related decision points—useful as a mining index into the narrative chapters.

Member views

Separate views of Senator Philip A. Hart; additional views of Senator Robert Morgan; additional views of Senator Howard H. Baker, Jr.; additional views of Senator Barry Goldwater; supplemental views of Senator Charles McC. Mathias, Jr. These record concurrence, emphasis, and dissent on evidence and recommendations.

Coverage Guide

Congo / Lumumba (1960–61)

Part III.A (+ chronology) for Eisenhower-era Congo policy and Lumumba plot reconstruction.

Cuba / Castro / MONGOOSE / underworld (1960–63 and related)

Part III.B–C for the densest operational and authorization narrative; pairs with Book I covert-action chapters for institutional context.

Trujillo and Diem (1961, 1963)

Parts III.D–E for Caribbean and Vietnam coup/assassination boundary cases.

Chile / Schneider (1970)

Part III.F as the assassination-focused Chile piece; use with Vol. 7 staff report Covert Action in Chile 1963–1973 for election and Allende-years programs.

Normative and statutory response (1975)

Parts IV–V, Appendix A, and member views for the contemporary ban-and-control agenda before the April 1976 final books.
