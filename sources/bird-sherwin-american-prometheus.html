Overview

American Prometheus is Kai Bird and Martin J. Sherwin’s biography of J. Robert Oppenheimer (1904–1967): theoretical physicist, builder of the Berkeley theoretical school, scientific director of Los Alamos, postwar government adviser and Institute for Advanced Study director, and the most prominent scientist destroyed by the McCarthy-era security system. Bird and Sherwin frame his life as triumph (quantum physics, wartime leadership, public intellectual stature) and tragedy (left associations, Chevalier episode, hydrogen-bomb opposition, Lewis Strauss’s vendetta, and the 1954 AEC hearing).

For this United States 1946–89 wiki the book is a core Cold War science-and-security spine: Trinity and Hiroshima aftermath, Acheson–Lilienthal / Baruch international control debates, AEC politics, FBI surveillance, the Super controversy, and the Gray Board hearing. Parts One–Two (childhood through 1942) are retained for continuity; Parts Three–Five plus Preface/Prologue/Epilogue are the wiki priority. Period-gate late family aftermath (Kitty, Toni, Frank’s Exploratorium) as post-career coda.

Front matter

Lines 1–208. TOC; title/dedication/praise; Knopf/Vintage front matter.

Preface

Lines 209–407. Opens on December 1953: Strauss hands Oppenheimer the AEC charges letter; the career-and-self-worth crisis that structures the biography’s tragic arc.

Prologue

Lines 408–551. Princeton funeral, 25 February 1967: six hundred mourners; “Oppie” as teacher, “father” of the bomb, national hero, then fallen figure—“Damn it, I happen to love this country.”

PART ONE

Chapter One — “He Received Every New Idea as Perfectly Beautiful”

Lines 556–1427. Early-century science revolution; Oppenheimer childhood as “unctuous, repulsively good little boy”; Ethical Culture / New York Jewish milieu and first intellectual formation.

Chapter Two — “His Separate Prison”

Lines 1428–1933. Harvard 1922 enrollment; Standish Hall; extreme physique and social isolation; early adult “separate prison” temperament.

Chapter Three — “I Am Having a Pretty Bad Time”

Lines 1934–2580. Post-Harvard emotional crisis (1926 letter); Cambridge difficulties and near-disastrous psychological strain after the protective undergraduate shield falls away.

Chapter Four — “I Find the Work Hard, Thank God, & Almost Pleasant”

Lines 2581–3119. Move to Göttingen (1926); quantum mecca of Lower Saxony; hard, almost pleasant work and scientific maturation under Born’s circle.

Chapter Five — “I Am Oppenheimer”

Lines 3120–3649. End of Göttingen year; homesickness and American chauvinism (New Mexico deserts); self-assertion as Oppenheimer among European peers (Rabi contrast).

Chapter Six — “Oppie”

Lines 3650–4260. Zurich productivity; Perro Caliente rhythm—intense work then New Mexico horseback renewal; 1931 letter on a restless, uncompromising world.

Chapter Seven — “The Nim Nim Boys”

Lines 4261–4887. Parents’ 1930 Pasadena visit; Depression-era family fortune after Julius’s 1928 retirement; Berkeley/Caltech “Nim Nim Boys” circle and politics vs. truth/beauty question.

PART TWO

Lines 4888–4891. Part divider: politics, love, and wartime recruitment (Chs. 8–14).

Chapter Eight — “In 1936 My Interests Began to Change”

Lines 4892–5633. Meeting Jean Tatlock (spring 1936); courtship; political awakening tied to personal life (Serber: Jean as truest love).

Chapter Nine — “[Frank] Clipped It Out and Sent It In”

Lines 5634–6290. Julius’s death (20 Sept 1937); Chevalier friendship and ambiguous Communist Party membership claims; brother Frank’s Party card episode.

Chapter Ten — “More and More Surely”

Lines 6291–6711. Nazi–Soviet pact (24 Aug 1939) and Poland invasion; Weisskopf on a decisive weekend turning Oppenheimer away from the Party; letter to Willie Fowler.

Chapter Eleven — “I’m Going to Marry a Friend of Yours, Steve”

Lines 6712–7294. Break with Tatlock; engagement near-misses; courtship and marriage path to Kitty (Serber on her advancing Robert’s career).

Chapter Twelve — “We Were Pulling the New Deal to the Left”

Lines 7295–7869. Popular Front / New Deal left organizing; teachers’ union and related Bay Area political work pulling reform leftward.

Chapter Thirteen — “The Coordinator of Rapid Rupture”

Lines 7870–8532. Early fission program role—“Coordinator of Rapid Rupture”; recruitment into the wartime bomb project apparatus.

Chapter Fourteen — “The Chevalier Affair”

Lines 8533–8811. Haakon Chevalier approach and the security episode that will haunt every later clearance fight.

PART THREE

Lines 8812–8815. Part divider: Los Alamos and the gadget (Chs. 15–22).

Chapter Fifteen — “He’d Become Very Patriotic”

Lines 8816–9615. Wartime patriotism and lab leadership persona; building the Los Alamos community.

Chapter Sixteen — “Too Much Secrecy”

Lines 9616–10178. Secrecy regime, compartmentation tensions, and scientific communication under military control.

Chapter Seventeen — “Oppenheimer Is Telling the Truth …”

Lines 10179–10767. Security interrogations and truthfulness contests inside the project’s clearance culture.

Chapter Eighteen — “Suicide, Motive Unknown”

Lines 10768–11025. Jean Tatlock’s death and its personal/security repercussions.

Chapter Nineteen — “Would You Like to Adopt Her?”

Lines 11026–11589. Family life at Los Alamos; children and domestic strain under wartime secrecy.

Chapter Twenty — “Bohr Was God, and Oppie Was His Prophet”

Lines 11590–11964. Niels Bohr’s visits; internationalist open-world vision versus military secrecy; Oppie as Bohr’s prophet.

Chapter Twenty-one — “The Impact of the Gadget on Civilization”

Lines 11965–12527. Interim Committee / target and use debates; scientists’ reckoning with civilization-scale effects.

Chapter Twenty-two — “Now We’re All Sons-of-Bitches”

Lines 12528–13442. Trinity test; Bainbridge’s line; path to Hiroshima and Nagasaki use.

PART FOUR

Lines 13443–13446. Part divider: postwar fame, AEC politics, and approaching doom (Chs. 23–33).

Chapter Twenty-three — “Those Poor Little People”

Lines 13447–13900. Immediate Hiroshima aftermath; Oppenheimer’s reaction to Japanese victims and public celebrity.

Chapter Twenty-four — “I Feel I Have Blood on My Hands”

Lines 13901–14468. Truman encounter and blood-on-hands remark; early postwar guilt and policy voice.

Chapter Twenty-five — “People Could Destroy New York”

Lines 14469–15143. International control, Acheson–Lilienthal lineage, and urban-destruction warnings in the new nuclear order.

Chapter Twenty-six — “Oppie Had a Rash and Is Now Immune”

Lines 15144–15919. Security clearance scrapes and the sense (among allies) that he had survived and was “immune”—a false calm.

Chapter Twenty-seven — “An Intellectual Hotel”

Lines 15920–16867. Directorship of the Institute for Advanced Study; Princeton as intellectual hotel and public platform.

Chapter Twenty-eight — “He Couldn’t Understand Why He Did It”

Lines 16868–17544. Personal contradictions, relationships, and self-sabotaging episodes as political enemies gather.

Chapter Twenty-nine — “I Am Sure That Is Why She Threw Things at Him”

Lines 17545–17985. Kitty, domestic turbulence, and the private costs of public pressure (FBI awareness, wiretap lore).

Chapter Thirty — “He Never Let On What His Opinion Was”

Lines 17986–18659. GAC / Super debates; studied ambiguity and the political price of opposing the hydrogen bomb.

Chapter Thirty-one — “Dark Words About Oppie”

Lines 18660–19662. Campaign of dark words from Strauss, Teller circle, and security bureaucrats; Kennan as intellectual ally after the Super fight.

Chapter Thirty-two — “Scientist X”

Lines 19663–19975. FBI/HUAC/Justice closing in (1950); Weinberg estrangement; “Scientist X” spy-hunt shadow over Oppenheimer.

Chapter Thirty-three — “The Beast in the Jungle”

Lines 19976–21007. Henry James story as personal omen; 1953 “two scorpions in a bottle” image; premonition before Strauss’s letter.

PART FIVE

Lines 21008–21011. Part divider: the 1954 inquisition and after (Chs. 34–40).

Chapter Thirty-four — “It Looks Pretty Bad, Doesn’t It?”

Lines 21012–21493. Refusal to resign; Nichols/Green charges machinery; Kafka Trial epigraph for the American inquisition.

Chapter Thirty-five — “I Fear That This Whole Thing Is a Piece of Idiocy”

Lines 21494–22721. Gray Board proceedings skewed from the outset; Strauss’s fear of defection/passport and McCarthy interference.

Chapter Thirty-six — “A Manifestation of Hysteria”

Lines 22722–23414. Defense witnesses (Bethe, Kennan, McCloy, Bush, Conant, et al.); McCloy to Eisenhower on inquiring into a Newton or Galileo.

Chapter Thirty-seven — “A Black Mark on the Escutcheon of Our Country”

Lines 23415–23970. Waiting for Gray Board judgment; Lilienthal’s verdict on public-service wisdom; FBI wiretaps of Oppenheimer’s despair.

Chapter Thirty-eight — “I Can Still Feel the Warm Blood on My Hands”

Lines 23971–24612. Aftermath letters; Rabi: the hearing achieved opponents’ aim—it destroyed him; public and private wreckage.

Chapter Thirty-nine — “It Was Really Like a Never-Never-Land”

Lines 24613–24979. St. John exile from 1954; separate prison renewed in Virgin Islands beauty; Hiilivirta on his humility.

Chapter Forty — “It Should Have Been Done the Day After Trinity”

Lines 24980–25647. Partial rehabilitation under Kennedy/Johnson; Fermi Award (Dec 1963) and Oppenheimer’s charity/courage remark to LBJ.

Epilogue — “There’s Only One Robert”

Lines 25648–25761. Kitty and Serber; Kitty’s death (1972); Frank’s Exploratorium; Peter in New Mexico; Toni’s clearance denial, depression, and 1977 suicide; Oppenheimer Beach.

Coverage Guide

Formation and European training (pre-wiki continuity)

Chs. 1–7: childhood, Harvard, Cambridge crisis, Göttingen, Caltech/Berkeley “Oppie”—needed to read later loyalty and temperament arguments.

Left politics, Tatlock, Kitty, Chevalier (continuity into clearance fights)

Chs. 8–14: 1936–42 political and personal web that the 1954 hearing will weaponize—essential background for security-case pages.

Los Alamos and the bomb (1942–45 bridge into wiki period)

Chs. 15–22: lab leadership, secrecy, Bohr, Interim Committee, Trinity, use of the bombs—pair with Groves Now It Can Be Told where that source is available in adjacent wikis.

Postwar adviser, IAS, Super, and siege (wiki core)

Chs. 23–33: blood-on-hands politics, international control, Institute directorship, hydrogen-bomb fight, FBI/HUAC, Strauss campaign—central 1945–53 material.

1954 hearing and aftermath (wiki core)

Chs. 34–40 + Preface/Prologue: AEC inquisition, Gray Board, destruction of public role, St. John exile, Fermi Award rehabilitation—McCarthy-era science-and-security centerpiece.

Epilogue (period gate)

Family aftermath into the 1970s: summarize only where completing Oppenheimer’s legacy; do not expand Toni/Frank stories into primary entity pages unless separately sourced for the 1946–89 frame.
