Overview

Steve Jobs is Isaacson’s full-life authorized biography, drawn from more than forty interviews with Jobs over two years plus more than a hundred interviews with family, friends, adversaries, and colleagues. Jobs encouraged candor, waived control over the manuscript, and did not read it in advance. The book is a continuous chronological narrative: Characters list; Introduction on how the book came to be; Chapters 1–42 from childhood adoption through legacy, spanning Apple’s founding, the Macintosh, the 1985 ouster, NeXT and Pixar, the 1996–97 return, consumer-electronics reinvention, illness, and death-adjacent reflection.

For this United States 1946–89 wiki the highest-value material is Chapters 1–18 (and related personal chapters): Paul and Clara Jobs in the postwar Bay Area, Homestead High and Wozniak, counterculture and Reed, Atari, Homebrew and Apple I/II, Markkula and the 1980 IPO, Xerox PARC and Lisa, Mac development and the 1984 launch, Sculley, Gates, and the 1985 board crisis, then NeXT’s late-1980s founding. Pixar purchase (1986) and early animation technology sit at the period edge. Chapters 19–42 carry substantial post-1989 spill (Toy Story 1995, Apple restoration 1997+, iMac, stores, iPod/iTunes, iPhone, iPad, cancer). Use post-1989 chapters only for continuity, later consequences of pre-1990 choices, or explicitly marked spill; prioritize pre-1990 U.S. personal-computing, Silicon Valley culture, and corporate-conflict arcs.

EPUB structure: Characters; Introduction; Chapters 1–42; residual notes/back matter in the extract tail of Chapter 42.

Narrative Source Flow

Characters

Lines 278–447. Dramatis personae spanning Jobs’s life: Al Alcorn, Gil Amelio, Bill Atkinson, Chrisann Brennan, Lisa Brennan-Jobs, Nolan Bushnell, Bill Campbell, Edwin Catmull, Kobun Chino, Lee Clow, Debi Coleman, Tim Cook, Eddy Cue, Andrea Cunningham, Michael Eisner, Larry Ellison, through Burrell Smith, Avie Tevanian, James Vincent, Ron Wayne, and Stephen Wozniak. Quick-reference cast for the continuous narrative.

Introduction: How This Book Came to Be

Lines 448–654. Isaacson recounts Jobs’s 2004 biography request after the Franklin and Einstein books; walks as Jobs’s preferred serious conversation; initial demurral then full cooperation with no manuscript control. New Year’s Eve 2009 Palo Alto call with Mona Simpson; cancer context; Henry V muse-of-fire frame opening a tale of two sets of parents and Silicon Valley.

Chapter 1 — Childhood: Abandoned and Chosen

Lines 655–1402. Paul Jobs leaves Coast Guard after WWII, marries Clara Hagopian in March 1946; adopts Steve; Mountain View/Los Altos craftsmanship upbringing; real-estate setback and family strain; school rebellion; Homestead High electronics and lasers. Abandoned-and-chosen theme structures the adoption story.

Chapter 2 — Odd Couple: The Two Steves

Lines 1403–1754. Friendship with Wozniak; Paul Jobs’s deal-making versus Jerry Wozniak’s Caltech/Lockheed engineering ethos. Garage work with Bill Fernandez; Blue Box phreak device establishes the template—Woz invents, Jobs packages and sells—for the Apple partnership.

Chapter 3 — The Dropout: Turn On, Tune In …

Lines 1755–2168. Chrisann Brennan romance; fruitarian diets, unblinking stare, LSD; Reed College path with Daniel Kottke; Zen and Eastern books (Suzuki, Yogananda, Trungpa). Later credits psychedelics and Zen with prioritizing creation and consciousness over money.

Chapter 4 — Atari and India: Zen and the Art of Game Design

Lines 2169–2656. February 1974 Atari job under Nolan Bushnell and Al Alcorn; Bushnell mentors acting in control. India pilgrimage for Neem Karoli Baba; illness and recovery; return with driven entrepreneurial attitude fusing games, Zen, and showmanship.

Chapter 5 — The Apple I: Turn On, Boot Up, Jack In …

Lines 2657–3203. Bay Area fusion of military electronics, hackers, LSD research (Engelbart), hippies, Free Speech politics, and self-fulfillment movements. Homebrew sharing ethic; Woz designs Apple I to give away; Jobs commercializes. Atlantic City fair; SOL-20 contrast teaches finished packaging.

Chapter 6 — The Apple II: Dawn of a New Age

Lines 3204–3729. Vision of fully packaged PC for non-hobbyists; Woz color/board genius; Mike Markkula joins with finance and distribution skill. McKenna: without Jobs the machine stays in hobby shops. Apple II as Woz masterpiece pushing Jobs toward a machine of his own.

Chapter 7 — Chrisann and Lisa: He Who Is Abandoned …

Lines 3730–3951. On-and-off Brennan relationship; pregnancy; Jobs’s denial and cold disengagement; Lisa born 1978. Barren Los Gatos house with Einstein and Maharaj-ji images; Halloween-as-Jesus. Personal abandonment theme mirrored in family life.

Chapter 8 — Xerox and Lisa: Graphical User Interfaces

Lines 3952–4311. Apple II sales boom; Apple III flop (May 1980). Xerox PARC bitmapping/GUI; Raskin advocacy. September 1980 reorganization strips Jobs of Lisa and R&D VP role—non-executive chairman, feels abandoned by Markkula and Scott.

Chapter 9 — Going Public: A Man of Wealth and Fame

Lines 4312–4506. 1977 valuation about 5,309toDecember1980IPO(mostoversubscribedsinceFord1956);1.79 billion year-end value. Kottke denied options. Shares 22to29; Jobs worth $256 million at twenty-five. Sixties idealism versus later careerism.

Chapter 10 — The Mac Is Born: You Say You Want a Revolution

Lines 4507–4811. Raskin’s mass-computer project renamed Macintosh; clash with Jobs over mouse, icons, power. Atkinson sides with Jobs; Raskin loses project. Scott’s layoffs; Markkula interim; Jobs gains full Mac rein.

Chapter 11 — The Reality Distortion Field: Playing by His Own Set of Rules

Lines 4812–5098. Tribble coins reality distortion field (Star Trek) for Jobs’s persuasion and impossible schedules. Hero/shithead binary; idea-appropriation pirouettes; A-player culture; abuse alongside fierce loyalty (Coleman).

Chapter 12 — The Design: Real Artists Simplify

Lines 5099–5466. Eichler modernism, car styling, Sony brochures, Aspen/Italian design influence. Case lines (Porsche not Ferrari); Mercedes detail model. Forty-five signatures engraved inside each Mac; work framed as art.

Chapter 13 — Building the Mac: The Journey Is the Reward

Lines 5467–5946. IBM PC August 1981 dissected and mocked; Welcome IBM ad. Time profile harshness and Lisa Brennan exposure. Engineers defy Jobs on disk drive (Sony vs Alps); he thanks them for disobeying.

Chapter 14 — Enter Sculley: The Pepsi Challenge

Lines 5947–6326. Markkula seeks president; Jobs not ready; Sculley courtship after Estridge unavailable. Pepsi/Apple visits; Mac price raised against team—Jobs later blames high price for slow sales and Microsoft’s rise. Control struggle begins.

Chapter 15 — The Launch: A Dent in the Universe

Lines 6327–6764. 1983 Dating Game with Gates predicting Mac software revenue while Microsoft shifts to IBM PC OS. Ridley Scott 1984 Super Bowl ad. Mac ships; Jobs scorns market research. Neither Raskin nor committees could have made the Mac.

Chapter 16 — Gates and Jobs: When Orbits Intersect

Lines 6765–7084. Binary-star rivalry of two 1955 dropouts; backgrounds contrasted. Mac demos; Gates doubts software cursor elegance. Jobs on Microsoft’s lack of taste; best products do not always win.

Chapter 17 — Icarus: What Goes Up …

Lines 7085–8236. Post-launch celebrity (Warhol, Haring, Jagger); unfurnished mansions. Board crisis; Sculley alliance collapses; spring 1985 showdown and ouster. Russia trip; exile mood—hibernate and reemerge thinking differently.

Chapter 18 — NeXT: Prometheus Unbound

Lines 8237–9224. Paul Berg gene-splicing talk sparks academic workstation company. Rand logo fight over yellow e. Beautiful costly hardware; Gates refuses software and parodies launch. 1989 sales about 400/month vs factory for 10,000; cash burn.

Chapter 19 — Pixar: Technology Meets Art

Lines 9225–9642. 1985 Kay walk to Lucasfilm computer division; Catmull and Lasseter; Jobs buys after Apple refuses. RenderMan limited; Disney CAPS. Wrong hardware bet, right art-plus-digital animation instinct.

Chapter 20 — A Regular Guy: Love Is Just a Four-Letter Word

Lines 9643–10248. Joan Baez romance and Dylan link; sister Mona Simpson reunion (Miyake box, later novel A Regular Guy); Tina Redse spiritual regret. Personal life in wilderness years.

Chapter 21 — Family Man: At Home with the Jobs Clan

Lines 10249–10870. October 1989 Stanford lecture; Laurene Powell front-row meet; marriage. Sparse bachelor party; children; Ellison as rich friend; anti-ostentation Buddhist lesson and no CEO security detail.

Chapter 22 — Toy Story: Buzz and Woody to the Rescue

Lines 10871–11207. Katzenberg partners Pixar for feature; 1993 half-film disaster and rewrite; later equal branding fight with Disney. Jobs on building brands/companies (Apple and Pixar). Primary events post-1989.

Chapter 23 — The Second Coming: What Rough Beast, Its Hour Come Round at Last …

Lines 11208–11643. NeXT hardware fails vs Sun; 1992 NeXTSTEP license. Apple Copland vaporware under Amelio; Be vs NeXT. Acquisition path; Jobs seeks Lasseter blessing to help Apple. 1996-97 reentry.

Chapter 24 — The Restoration: The Loser Now Will Be Later to Win

Lines 11644–12473. Post-forty flourishing; Ellison strategy of board seat until Amelio stumbles. Stock-sale flap; Microsoft detente speech; one-day 33 percent stock jump, about $830 million cap—back from the grave. 1997 center.

Chapter 25 — Think Different: Jobs as iCEO

Lines 12474–12929. Lee Clow and Think Different; crazy ones; Dreyfuss voice chosen over Jobs. Counterculture brand restored; one more thing; Think Profit; FY1998 profit $309 million.

Chapter 26 — Design Principles: The Studio of Jobs and Ive

Lines 12930–13220. Jony Ive nearly quits; great-products philosophy retains him. Craftsman father; simplicity as conquered complexity. Credit tensions; Jobs drives design through resistance.

Chapter 27 — The iMac: Hello (Again)

Lines 13221–13549. May 1998 all-in-one consumer iMac about $1,200; design/engineering unity; Jobs fury over tray button. Translucent Bondi blue hit; slot-vs-tray choice delays rip-burn then forces music leap.

Chapter 28 — CEO: Still Crazy after All These Years

Lines 13550–13889. Operational focus: cut lines, outsource manufacturing, crush inventory. Tim Cook hired 1998. Miyake uniform fails; turtleneck remains. Drops interim title to ovation.

Chapter 29 — Apple Stores: Genius Bars and Siena Sandstone

Lines 13890–14241. Control retail experience vs big-box spiffs; Ron Johnson; Drexler prototype advice; secret Cupertino store lab. Glass cube minimalism. 2000s spill.

Chapter 30 — The Digital Hub: From iTunes to the iPod

Lines 14242–14818. Top 100 retreats; pick three priorities. 2001 digital hub; iTunes; iPod; Gates ‘only for Macintosh?’ Post-1989 music-device arc.

Chapter 31 — The iTunes Store: I’m the Pied Piper

Lines 14819–15456. Legal downloads vs Napster-era piracy; label talks; Iovine on Apple turnkey versus Sony silos. iTunes Store and trusted payments platform. 2002-03+ spill.

Chapter 32 — Music Man: The Sound Track of His Life

Lines 15457–15985. iPod-as-identity culture; Jobs’s library; Dylan boxed set; Yo-Yo Ma Bach and funeral promise. Music as lifelong aesthetic core.

Chapter 33 — Pixar’s Friends: … and Foes

Lines 15986–16642. Jobs lets Lasseter lead at calm Pixar vs volatile Apple. Eisner wars; Iger merger; Catmull/Lasseter at Disney animation. Building great companies. Late spill.

Chapter 34 — Twenty-first-century Macs: Setting Apple Apart

Lines 16643–16935. iBook clam, G4 Cube (MoMA); Motorola fight; secret Intel switch planning. $1 salary versus large grants; handicapped-parking contradiction of rebel and tycoon.

Chapter 35 — Round One: Memento Mori

Lines 16936–17408. 2003 pancreatic shadow on kidney scan; delay and diets; Stanford commencement (dots, love, death). Integrated versus modular philosophy; weak partnering DNA admission.

Chapter 36 — The iPhone: Three Revolutionary Products in One

Lines 17409–17780. Phone threat to iPod; ROKR failure; multi-touch from tablet work; P1/P2 paths. iPhone as three products in one; Kay Dynabook note. 2005-07 spill.

Chapter 37 — Round Two: The Cancer Recurs

Lines 17781–18312. 2008 progression, pain, weight loss, diet obsession. Board/SEC disclosure issues. Brief recovery and product push toward iPad year.

Chapter 38 — The iPad: Into the Post-PC Era

Lines 18313–19069. Tablet ambitions deferred for phone multi-touch; 2010 launch; closed ecosystem; textbooks and publishers. Post-PC consumer computing spill.

Chapter 39 — New Battles: And Echoes of Old Ones

Lines 19070–19587. Android/Google betrayal rant; open vs closed; Flash ban; app control controversies; Beatles on iTunes. Echoes of 1980s platform control fights.

Chapter 40 — To Infinity: The Cloud, the Spaceship, and Beyond

Lines 19588–20066. iPad 2; magnetic cover; yacht as anti-death project; Laurene anniversary; Andrea Cunningham ‘journey is the reward’—Jobs says he learned some things.

Chapter 41 — Round Three: The Twilight Struggle

Lines 20067–20879. Reed’s graduation as goal; family; Obama Valley dinner planning; medical leave; Cook succession. ‘I’ve done all that I can do.‘

Chapter 42 — Legacy: The Brightest Heaven of Invention

Lines 20880–end. Personality-product unity: intensity, end-to-end integration, hero/shithead binary, perfectionism, artistry, control. From 1984 Mac to iPad as one philosophy. Closing legacy judgment; notes/back matter in extract tail.

Coverage Guide

Postwar Bay Area youth and counterculture-to-electronics path (pre-1990 priority)

Chapters 1–4: adoption and Paul/Clara Jobs after 1946, Homestead High, Wozniak partnership, Reed dropout, Zen/LSD, Atari, India. Core for Silicon Valley social history and the human capital of the personal-computer industry.

Apple founding through IPO and Lisa/Mac origins (pre-1990 priority)

Chapters 5–12: Homebrew, Apple I/II, Markkula, Chrisann/Lisa personal crisis, Xerox PARC GUI narrative, 1980 IPO, Raskin Mac project, reality distortion field, industrial design. Spine of U.S. personal-computing business history 1976–84.

Macintosh launch, IBM/Microsoft rivalry, Sculley, ouster (pre-1990 priority)

Chapters 13–17: IBM PC response, Sculley courtship, 1984 ad and Mac launch, Gates relationship, 1985 crisis and departure. Essential for 1980s computer industry structure and celebrity-entrepreneur culture.

NeXT and Pixar beginnings (period edge)

Chapters 18–19: NeXT workstation bet (mid/late 1980s into 1989 sales failure); Lucasfilm computer division purchase and early Pixar hardware/CAPS path. Pre-1990 corporate facts usable; animation triumphs are later.

Personal life across the divide

Chapters 7, 20–21: Brennan/Lisa; Baez, Mona Simpson, Tina Redse; Laurene Powell (from 1989). Use for biography and family dimensions of tech entrepreneurship; marriage and children continue past 1989.

Post-1989 spill (continuity only)

Chapters 22–42: Toy Story and Disney wars; Amelio/NeXT acquisition; Think Different, Ive, iMac; retail stores; iPod/iTunes; iPhone/iPad; cancer; legacy. Mine only when tracing consequences of integrated hardware-software philosophy, design culture, or platform control doctrines formed before 1990. Do not treat 2000s product narrative as primary 1946–89 evidence.
