Overview

The Chief is David Nasaw’s full-life biography of William Randolph Hearst, built with new access to Hearst Corporation materials, San Simeon oral histories, and Joseph P. Kennedy papers among many archives. Nasaw tracks the making of a self-fashioned media sovereign: mining-heir childhood; Harvard dropout turned Examiner proprietor; New York Journal spectacle and the Spanish-American War; Progressive-era candidacies and Congress; World War I neutrality/treason fights; 1920s studio, collecting, and Marion Davies Hollywood court; Depression finance crisis; Roosevelt support then anti–New Deal/anti-Communist crusades; European contacts including Hitler; the late-1930s fall; Citizen Kane; and managed old age until death in 1951.

For this United States 1946–89 wiki the book’s core is thin but real: Chapters 36 and Epilogue (post-1945 old age, estate, San Simeon gift path) plus late cultural afterlife of the Hearst myth. Parts I–VIII and most of IX are continuity from earlier U.S. period wikis—mine those fully in the 1866–1919 and 1920–45 sister pages. Period-gate hard on pre-1946 narrative except where needed to finish a story already open after 1945.

PDF structure: TOC; copyright/LC; Acknowledgments; Preface; Parts I–IX with Chapters 1–36; Epilogue; Notes; Index; About the Author.
